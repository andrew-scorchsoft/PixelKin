# AI writing tells

A quick pass when reviewing non-fiction prose. If the text trips several of
these, it reads as generic machine-assembled writing — fix it before
delivering. The point is not to ban these patterns reflexively, but to make
sure each one is a deliberate choice, not a default.

## Voice and tone

- Overly polished but strangely personality-free.
- Sounds like a generic explainer, a LinkedIn post, or a motivational article.
- Too balanced, too safe, unwilling to take a clear position.
- Emotionally warm but not genuinely personal or specific.
- Uses "thought leadership" language without real insight.
- Feels like it was written by the internet average rather than by a person.

## Structure

- Every section follows the same neat pattern: intro → explanation → example →
  takeaway.
- Too much signposting: "firstly", "secondly", "finally", "in conclusion".
- Repeated rhetorical questions: "So why does this matter?"
- Conclusions that merely restate the introduction.
- Paragraphs too symmetrical in length and rhythm.
- Too many three-part lists: "clear, concise, and compelling".

## Phrases and patterns

- Filler openers and connectors: "It's important to note…", "In today's
  fast-paced world…", "As technology continues to evolve…", "This highlights
  the importance of…", "Ultimately…", "Let's dive in…".
- Repeated "not X, but Y" constructions.
- Too many "can help" claims.
- Overused abstract verbs: *unlock, empower, elevate, transform, leverage,
  supercharge, streamline*.
- Overused urgency words: *crucial, vital, essential, pivotal*.
- AI-flavoured words in clusters: *delve, landscape, realm, tapestry, robust,
  seamless, nuanced, testament, navigate, foster*.

## Specificity

- Broad claims with no concrete example.
- "Research shows" or "experts agree" with no named source.
- Avoids names, dates, numbers, places, tools, prices, real incidents.
- Fake examples that could apply to anyone.
- Lacks messy details, trade-offs, exceptions, lessons learned.
- Sounds confident without showing the evidence behind the claim.

## Argument quality

- States obvious truths as if they were insights.
- Avoids saying anything a reader could reasonably disagree with.
- Repeats the thesis too explicitly.
- Explains concepts without adding judgement, experience, or interpretation.
- Generic positivity: "challenges are opportunities for growth".
- Makes everything sound smoother, easier, or more inspiring than it is.

## Sentence rhythm

- Too many similarly shaped sentences.
- Repeated templates: "By doing X, you can Y", "When teams X, they can Y",
  "Whether you're X or Y, Z matters".
- Too many short dramatic lines stacked together.
- Em dashes overused beyond the author's normal style.
- Grammar is perfect but the prose feels sterile.

## The human check

- Could another author have written this exact paragraph?
- Does this sound like something the author would actually say?
- Is there a concrete example from real experience?
- Is there a clear opinion, not just a balanced summary?
- Can the details be defended if a reader challenges them?
- Has the text said something useful, specific, or memorable?
