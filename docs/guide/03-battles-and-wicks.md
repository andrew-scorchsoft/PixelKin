## The battle menu

Battles are turn-based, one kin each side. Your lead is the top kin in your KIN list.

| Command | What it does |
|---------|--------------|
| FIGHT | Choose one of your kin's moves. |
| LAMP | Throw the vesperlamp, or a charge, to catch a wild kin. (Wild battles only.) |
| BAG | Use a balm or other item. |
| KIN | Switch to another kin. The switch costs your turn. |
| RUN | Leave a wild battle. You can't run from a trainer. |

**Move details.** In the FIGHT menu, press **Right** to open a small panel with the highlighted
move's type, power, accuracy and uses left. Against a kin you've caught before it also tells you
whether the move is super-effective or resisted. Press **Left** to hide it again.

## Types in brief

Every kin and every move has a type. There are ten: **Ember, Tide, Verdant, Stone, Storm,
Frost, Solar, Lunar, Light** and **Dark**. A super-effective hit does double damage; a resisted
one does half.

The early triangle is easy to remember: **Ember beats Verdant, Verdant beats Tide, Tide beats
Ember.** Three facts catch people out later:

- **Storm moves do nothing at all to Stone kin.**
- **Lunar moves do nothing at all to Dark kin.**
- **Solar and Lunar** hit each other super-effectively, and so do **Light and Dark**.

A kin with two types takes both into account, and a move that matches its user's own type hits
harder. The full table is in [[type-chart|Types & Matchups]].

## Experience and levels

Knocking out a kin earns experience; so does **catching** one. Trainer battles pay half as much
again. In the first hours, battles against low-level kin pay a little extra, so the opening
never drags.

With **EXP share** on (the default), the kin that landed the final blow keeps the full amount,
any other kin that fought takes half, and the rest of the party gets a quarter. You can turn it
off in Settings.

## Trainers

Trainers stand on the routes and in towns. Many watch the road: if one spots you, they walk up
and the battle begins. Each trainer battles once, then steps aside. A few spots offer repeat
bouts on purpose (see [[making-wicks|Making Wicks]]).

**Lampwardens** keep the eight Lumenaries. None of them simply waits to be fought: each has an
errand for you first, and the bond-test comes at the end of it.

## Wicks

Money in Vesperholm is **wicks**: waxed, brass-capped lamp-wicks that everyone always needs one
more of. You start with 250.

- **Trainers pay** when you beat them. The bigger the trainer, the bigger the purse. Wild kin
  pay nothing.
- **Quests** often end with a purse, and **wick-purses** lie hidden along the early roads.
- **Valuables** (Wax Cakes, Moth-amber, Starglass Shards and others) are worth nothing to use and
  good money at any counter.
- **Selling.** Any shop buys priced items back at half their price.

Resting is always free.

## When your party faints

If every kin in your party faints, a kind light carries you to the **last place you rested**
(an inn, a home, a fireside). Your kin are healed. The light keeps a small tithe of your wicks as
thanks:

- **10% of your purse** for the first loss,
- **halved** if the same foe beats you again in a row (5%, then 2.5%),
- **nothing at all** from the fourth loss in a row to the same foe.

Two limits keep it gentle:

- **A ceiling that grows with you:** never more than 300 wicks, plus 300 for each Gleam you hold
  (2,700 with all eight).
- **A floor:** the tithe never takes your purse below 300 wicks.

Beating that foe resets the count.

| Your purse | Gleams held | 1st loss | 2nd in a row | 3rd | 4th or more |
|-----------:|:-----------:|---------:|-------------:|----:|------------:|
| 250 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1,200 | 1 | 120 | 60 | 30 | 0 |
| 6,000 | 3 | 600 | 300 | 150 | 0 |
| 20,000 | 5 | 1,800 | 1,000 | 500 | 0 |

**Don't want to lose anything?** Turn on **Settings → Cannot die**. Your party is healed where it
stands, nothing is taken, and any story battle simply waits for you to try again.

## Tips for a smoother road

- **Rest before a Lampwarden.** Every Lumenary town has a free rest close by.
- **Catch a second and third kin early.** A party that covers more than one type makes every
  warden easier.
- **Mind the trainer purses.** Beaten trainers pay well enough that you rarely need to grind for
  money if you take on the ones you pass.
- **Lost?** Check the NEXT panel in the pause menu, or find Andrew on a fence.
