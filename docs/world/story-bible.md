# PixelKin — Story Bible: *The Long Dusk*

> The canonical narrative, lore, and cast for PixelKin's first region. Original to
> PixelKin; a nod to the cartridge-era monster-collecting genre, a copy of nothing.
> Read alongside [`atlas.md`](./atlas.md) (the world map) and
> [`README.md`](./README.md) (conventions). All names, places, kin, and abilities
> here are original per the copyright rules in [`../../VISION.md`](../../VISION.md);
> see the **Originality audit** at the end.

---

## 1. The pitch, in one breath

> *A land where night fell and never fully lifted — and the only way to bring back
> the dawn is to gather the scattered light, kin by kin, lantern by lantern.*

**Vesperholm** is a crescent of nine valleys cradled around a darkened mountain. The
stars overhead — which Vesperholm folk have always believed are *anchored* to gleaming
kin on the ground — have begun to wink out, one constellation at a time. With each lost
star a town's lamps gutter and a luminous kin vanishes. You are a young **lamp-tender's
apprentice** who sets out, on the rite of passage every Vesperholm child dreams of, to
walk the valleys, befriend kin, and relight the sky before the long night becomes
permanent.

The feeling we are selling is **lanterns in the dark**: cosy and a little melancholy,
warm windows in cold villages, the small heroism of relighting one constellation so the
next stretch of road becomes visible. Never grim — every long night here is a *promise
of a brighter morning*. The whole region leans into PixelKin's signature
night-blue-and-cyan palette (`night #0b1026`, `deepBlue #13205a`, `diamond #9fe7ff`),
so the art does the emotional work the moment the title fades in.

## 2. The world and its belief: the Skyweave

Vesperholm has always lived by the **Skyweave** — the old, half-folklore-half-fact
belief that the constellations are tied to **gleaming kin** on the ground, and that as
long as people *tend the light* (festivals, lighthouses, hearths, the dusk-bells) the
cycle of dusk and dawn keeps turning. For generations this simply worked, and so the
region is sleepy, sure of itself, and built around light: lantern festivals, observatory
shrines, lighthouses, and the warm habit of leaving a lamp lit for travellers.

A traveller carries a **vesperlamp** — a small brass clamshell lantern, handed down or
gifted at the start of the rite. It is the in-world heart of the whole game: it catches
and holds the light you restore, grows brighter as you progress, and (mechanically) is
how your traversal abilities and your map-light are expressed. It is a quiet, affectionate
nod to the handheld the player is holding — a little glowing device you carry everywhere,
that lights up the world the more you play.

The journey itself is **the Wayfaring**: the coming-of-age tradition where a young person
of age sets out to *"complete the map"* — visit every valley, befriend its kin, learn its
tale, and earn the standing of a true Vesperholm wanderer. Completing the Wayfaring is
how you become an adult here. The map is, very literally, the adventure.

## 3. Story arc

**Setup.** The game opens in **Tinderwick**, a cosy coastal village at the blue hour, when
the sky can't decide between day and night. A retired star-tender — your mentor — gives
you your vesperlamp and a starter kin, and sends you off on your Wayfaring.

**Inciting incident.** On the first night of your journey, a constellation *winks out* in
the sky, and at the same moment a luminous kin vanishes from a nearby valley and the
town's lamps gutter to a dim ember. It has happened before, in scattered places — but it
is getting worse, and faster. Someone, or something, is *eating the dark into the world*.

**Rising conflict.** The cause is the **Hollowing** — not villains in capes, but a quiet,
growing movement of ordinary, frightened people. Their leader, the former astronomer
**Warden Còr**, reasoned that since light always fades and every dawn only leads to
another dusk, the *kindest* thing is to let the long night come gently — no more loss, no
more grief, the world held forever in soft, painless dark. The Hollowing harvest the light
from luminous kin into **null-lanterns** (the kin are *put to sleep*, never harmed —
PixelKin stays cosy), believing they are sparing everyone the pain of the cycle. They are
a genuine threat precisely because they are *sympathetic and half-right*: the cycles **do**
cause loss. Some valleys even welcome them.

**The journey as the plot.** The eight honours you collect are not trophies. Each one is a
**relit anchor-constellation**. As you travel the outer rim of the crescent valley to
valley — befriending kin, helping towns the Hollowing have "quieted," and earning each
Lampwarden's trust — you light the sky back up one constellation at a time, and the world
physically brightens around you.

**Climax.** With all eight constellations relit, the completed **Skyweave Crown** casts
enough combined starlight to part the dark fog sealing the central mountain, the **Umbral
Spire**. There, Còr has built a **Great Null** aimed at the region's last anchoring star,
the **Keystar** — the one whose light keeps any of the others able to rekindle at all. The
climax is a race against a sunrise that may never come: relight the Keystar before the
Hollowing snuff it forever.

**Resolution.** You don't defeat Còr by force — you *out-remember* him. Through the kin and
the lights you've gathered, you prove that a cycle of loss is still worth more than a flat,
painless forever-dark; that the dawn is worth the dusk. The long night breaks. Vesperholm
wakes into its first true sunrise in years — and the ending is deliberately bittersweet and
warm: the cycle resumes, which means dusk will come again, and *that is exactly the point*.
Còr, undone, is not destroyed but quietly offered a place among the star-tenders again.

**Post-game.** The relit sky changes which kin appear (day-forms emerge); previously
dark folds of the valleys open up; the **Dawnstead** epilogue town blooms in full daylight.

## 4. The Lumenary & the Gleam (our "gym / badge", made original)

There are no gyms. There are **Lumenaries** — buildings that are half observatory, half
shrine, one in each major town, each tending a single **anchor-constellation**. The keeper
of a Lumenary is a **Lampwarden**: the town's strongest trainer of its associated kin and
the keeper of its constellation's flame.

To earn a town's honour, you face its Lampwarden in a battle of bonded kin. Winning does
**not** mean you beat them into submission — it means the Lampwarden *vouches that your
bond with kin is strong and steady enough to hold light under pressure*, and so they let
you **relight their constellation**. The honour you receive is a **Gleam** — a mote of
true, self-sustaining starlight caught in your vesperlamp.

> **Why a player travels for all eight.** Each relit constellation literally *adds light to
> the world*: it appears overhead in the night sky, the overworld map brightens in that
> region, night-only kin behaviour shifts, and new lamp-lit paths become visible. Collecting
> Gleams is **diegetic progress you can look up and see** — not an abstract trophy shelf.
> Eight Gleams assemble the **Skyweave Crown**, the only thing that can reopen the Spire.

## 5. The central locked region: the Umbral Spire

The **Umbral Spire** is the darkened mountain at the centre of the crescent; every valley
sits on its slopes like numbers on a clock-face. At its peak is the **Ninth Lantern**, a
long-dead Lumenary, and within it the **Keystar**.

**Why it's sealed.** The Spire is ringed by the **Penumbra** — a band of unnatural,
blinding dark fog so thick that kin won't enter and travellers lose their way within
minutes. The Penumbra is *fed by the eight darkened constellations*: with no light
overhead, the central dark has nothing to push back against it. You can see the Spire from
everywhere and reach it from nowhere — the genre's "central plateau you have to earn your
way into," reframed as **darkness itself**.

**How it opens.** Each **Gleam** relights one anchor-constellation, and each relit
constellation *burns back one wedge of the Penumbra*. Visually, the ring of dark fog
recedes one slice at a time as the **Skyweave Crown** fills in overhead. Only when all
eight Gleams are earned does the Crown complete, casting enough combined starlight to part
the Penumbra entirely and reveal the **four ascending roads** up the Spire.

**The multi-directional flow.** Vesperholm is built as a *ring around the Spire*. Early
game you travel the outer rim, valley to valley, because the Penumbra blocks every shortcut
to the centre. Once the Spire opens, its **four cardinal roads connect inward from the
north / south / east / west valleys at once**, and a relit waystation at the crater's heart
becomes the late-game fast-travel nexus. The world *opens up and speeds up* exactly when
you've earned it — the slow outer loop collapses into a fast four-way hub.

## 6. Lantern Gifts (our "field moves", made original)

Traversal abilities are unified by a single fantasy: **kinds of light your vesperlamp
learns**, each taught by a kin you befriend as you restore the world. Because the wild dark
of Vesperholm is genuinely impassable without light, each Gift opens new map areas. (Full
gating wiring is in [`atlas.md`](./atlas.md).)

| Lantern Gift | What it does | Why it gates the map | Opens |
|--------------|--------------|----------------------|-------|
| **Glimmerstep** | Sheds a soft glow that lets you enter shaded caves and woods "too dark to walk." | Without your own light the wild dark scatters kin and hides paths. | Lowleaf Hollow interiors, Cinderhead Mine, unlit caves. |
| **Tidecall** | Parts moon-tides so you can cross shallow night-water to lantern-buoys and islets. | The tides answer moonlight; only a lit lamp can ask them to part. | Pearlmoor shoals, island shrines, flooded routes. |
| **Emberward** | Burns a path through *coldfog*, the Hollowing's creeping mist. | Coldfog snuffs ordinary flame; only a tended ember pushes through. | Frostbound passes, Penumbra-adjacent edges, the Pale Vault approach. |
| **Updraft Kite** | A storm-kin lifts you on warm thermals to scale terraces and glide short gaps. | Terraces are sheer; you need a kin that rides the storm-light to climb. | Galehigh Terraces, high overlooks, sky-shrine ledges. |
| **Sunsketch** | Releases a stored "pocket of daylight" that blooms shut night-flowers into bridges. | Some bridges are living sun-vines that only open under daylight, lost since the long night. | Sunken Solarium, day-locked garden routes, solar puzzles. |
| **Starreach** *(late)* | Draws down faint starlight to step across short voids of pure dark. | Only a near-complete Skyweave gives enough starlight to "stand on nothing." | Final Penumbra gaps, the Umbral Spire ascent. |

## 7. The antagonist: the Hollowing

- **What they are.** A quiet cult of stillness — ordinary, frightened, grieving people who
  have concluded that the kindest thing is to end the painful cycle of light and loss by
  letting a gentle, permanent dark settle over the world.
- **Their leader, Warden Còr.** A brilliant former astronomer who lost something to the
  turning of the cycles and turned that grief into a philosophy. He is courteous, sad, and
  *persuasive* — never a cackling villain. The tragedy is that he is partly right.
- **What they do.** Harvest the light from luminous kin into **null-lanterns** ("putting kin
  to sleep," never killing), darkening constellations to thin the world's pain. They are
  genuinely helping in some valleys (no more loss), which is what makes them dangerous.
- **Why they're a real stake without being cruel.** A world without light slowly loses the
  ability to *wake up at all* — a painless dark that is also a quiet death of wonder. The
  drama is moral, not violent.

This matches the tournament's strongest consensus: **every** finalist independently chose a
sympathetic "well-meaning modernizer / extractor," not a crime syndicate. PixelKin's first
antagonist is grief dressed as mercy.

## 8. The celestial-calendar mechanic (the spine)

Day/night and "version-exclusive" creatures — genre staples — become the *structure* of the
whole story rather than a gimmick:

- **Relit constellations change the world.** Each Gleam visibly brightens its region of the
  overworld and shifts which kin appear and how they behave there.
- **A long-night-to-dawn arc.** The global lighting of the world warms gradually from the
  opening blue hour toward the final true sunrise — the player can *feel* the dawn coming.
- **Day-forms and night-forms.** Some kin only appear (or only evolve) under certain skies;
  the post-game dawn unlocks day-forms as a fresh collecting hook.

## 10. The nod, made original

- **Eight leaders + a central plateau you earn your way into** — the genre's bones — but
  each "badge" is a *relit constellation* that visibly changes the sky and unlocks map
  light, and the central plateau is sealed by *darkness itself*, burned back wedge by wedge.
- **"Gym battles"** become earning a **Gleam**: the Lampwarden *vouches you understand their
  town's light*. Same eight-honour cadence, the emotional logic of belonging instead of
  conquest.
- **Field moves** become **Lantern Gifts**, unified by one fantasy (kinds of light from your
  growing lamp) rather than a grab-bag of unrelated tricks.
- **The evil team** becomes the **Hollowing** — sympathetic, ideological, non-cartoonish,
  never lethal.
- **Day/night & version-exclusives** become a **celestial calendar** wired straight into the
  spine of the story.

## 11. Originality audit (per `VISION.md`)

Checked against the non-negotiable rules in [`../../VISION.md`](../../VISION.md):

- **No existing creatures, names, characters, places, or logos.** Every proper noun here —
  *Vesperholm, Tinderwick, the Skyweave, the Lumenary, a Gleam, the vesperlamp, Lantern
  Gifts, the Umbral Spire, the Penumbra, the Keystar, the Hollowing, Warden Còr,* and all
  eight Lampwardens — is original to PixelKin. None is a recognisable rename of an existing
  franchise's element, and no kin is described as resembling another franchise's creature.
- **No copied art, music, text, or maps.** All assets are to be generated from original
  briefs via the repo skills; this document describes by **genre and mood only**.
- **No trademarked terms** appear in names, lore, or the vocabulary (creatures are *kin*;
  arenas are *Lumenaries*; badges are *Gleams*; field moves are *Lantern Gifts*).
- **Describe by genre, not by brand.** Maintained throughout.
- **When in doubt, more original, not less.** The light-economy, the "progress written in
  the sky," and the grief-as-mercy antagonist are deliberately distinctive.
